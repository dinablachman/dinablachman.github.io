This research presents a novel framework for regulating artificial intelligence systems, particularly in contexts where AI decisions have significant real-world consequences.

<!--more-->

## Abstract

As artificial intelligence systems become increasingly integrated into critical decision-making processes across healthcare, finance, criminal justice, and other high-stakes domains, the need for comprehensive regulatory frameworks has become paramount. This paper proposes a multi-layered approach to AI regulation that balances innovation with accountability.

## Problem Statement

Current regulatory approaches to AI systems are fragmented and often reactive rather than proactive. The lack of standardized frameworks creates uncertainty for developers, deployers, and affected communities, potentially leading to inconsistent outcomes and reduced public trust in AI technologies.

## Methodology

Our framework is built on three core pillars:

1. **Risk-based Classification**: AI systems are categorized based on their potential impact and likelihood of harm
2. **Transparency Requirements**: Mandatory disclosure of system capabilities, limitations, and decision-making processes
3. **Accountability Mechanisms**: Clear lines of responsibility and recourse for AI-related decisions

## Key Findings

- Risk-based classification significantly improves regulatory efficiency
- Transparency requirements enhance public trust without stifling innovation
- Accountability mechanisms reduce bias and improve system reliability

## Implications

This framework provides a practical foundation for policymakers, technologists, and stakeholders to develop more effective AI governance structures. The research contributes to the growing body of work on AI ethics and regulation.

## Future Work

- Longitudinal studies on framework implementation
- International harmonization of regulatory approaches
- Development of automated compliance monitoring tools
